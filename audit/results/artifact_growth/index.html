What a dot is

Fire detections from the MODIS Aqua satellite (afternoon pass), merged into fire events when they are within 2 km and 2 days of each other. Each square is a 0.25° cell, about 28 km across, coloured by the largest cluster it has a fire in.

How fires join

Two fires belong to the same cluster when they are closer than the joining distance, directly or through a chain of other fires. Through the season, the distance is twice the typical spacing of that year's fires (14–23 km), so years with more or fewer fires compare fairly.

Colours

Clusters are ranked by size at each moment. When two clusters merge, the merged one takes the top colour, so a region can switch colour from one step to the next.

Joining up

A year "joins up" when the largest cluster first holds 40–60% of the year's fires (the average of the three dates). The shuffled-dates date uses the same fires with their start dates mixed up, which keeps the map but removes the timing.
